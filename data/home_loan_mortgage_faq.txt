FINANCIAL BANK — HOME LOAN AND MORTGAGE APPLICATION RAG KNOWLEDGE BASE |
FAQ DOCUMENT Document purpose: Answer common customer questions about
applying for a home loan or mortgage. Scope note: This is general
informational content for a fictional financial institution. Mortgage
products, eligibility, rates, fees, down-payment requirements,
timelines, and documentation vary by lender, borrower, property, and
jurisdiction. This document is not a credit decision, loan offer, legal
advice, or financial advice.

Q1. How do I apply for a home loan or mortgage? A: Customers can usually
begin through the lender’s website, a mortgage loan officer, or a branch
if mortgage services are offered there. The process typically includes
an initial inquiry, application, document submission, credit and income
review, property evaluation, underwriting, and closing if approved. The
lender will explain its current process and requirements.

Q2. What information is needed for a mortgage application? A: Applicants
are commonly asked for identity and contact information, employment and
income details, assets, existing debts, information about the property,
and permission for required credit checks. Co-borrowers may need to
provide similar information. Exact requirements depend on the loan
program and lender.

Q3. What documents should I prepare? A: Commonly requested documents may
include recent pay statements, W-2s or tax returns, bank or investment
statements, identification, employment details, records of other debts,
and purchase-contract information when buying a home. Self-employed
applicants may need additional income and business records. The lender
will provide a tailored document checklist.

Q4. What is mortgage prequalification? A: Prequalification is an early
estimate of potential borrowing capacity based on information the
applicant provides and the lender’s process. It may be less detailed
than preapproval and is not a final loan commitment. Customers should
ask the lender what checks were completed and what the result does—and
does not—confirm.

Q5. What is mortgage preapproval? A: Preapproval is a preliminary
assessment of a borrower’s ability to qualify, often involving review of
financial information and a credit check. It is not a guarantee of final
approval. Final approval can depend on verification of documents,
underwriting, the property’s appraisal, title review, and other
conditions.

Q6. What credit score do I need? A: Minimum credit-score requirements
vary by lender and loan program, and a score alone does not determine
approval. Lenders may also assess credit history, income, debts, assets,
down payment, and property details. The customer should ask the lender
which programs may fit their circumstances.

Q7. How much down payment do I need? A: The required down payment
depends on the loan program, property, occupancy, borrower
qualifications, and lender requirements. Some programs may allow lower
down payments, while others may require more. A lower down payment can
affect monthly costs and may result in mortgage insurance or other
requirements. The lender should confirm applicable terms.

Q8. What mortgage interest rates are available? A: Mortgage rates change
over time and depend on factors such as the loan type, term, market
conditions, credit profile, down payment, property, and whether the rate
is locked. The chatbot must not quote a rate unless it comes from an
approved, current lender source and includes relevant assumptions and
timing. Customers should request a current Loan Estimate or official
rate quote.

Q9. What is the difference between a fixed-rate and adjustable-rate
mortgage? A: With a fixed-rate mortgage, the interest rate generally
stays the same for the agreed loan term. With an adjustable-rate
mortgage, the rate may change according to the loan’s terms and index
after any initial fixed period. Payment changes and caps depend on the
specific product documents, which customers should review carefully.

Q10. What is the difference between principal, interest, taxes, and
insurance? A: Principal is the amount borrowed that is repaid over time.
Interest is the cost of borrowing. A monthly housing payment may also
include property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, and
applicable association dues or escrow items. The exact payment
components depend on the loan and property.

Q11. What is debt-to-income ratio (DTI)? A: Debt-to-income ratio
compares qualifying monthly debt payments with qualifying gross monthly
income, as calculated by the lender. Lenders use DTI as one factor in
assessing repayment ability, alongside credit, assets, loan terms, and
other criteria. Calculation rules and acceptable thresholds vary by
program.

Q12. What fees can come with a mortgage? A: Mortgage costs may include
application or origination charges, appraisal, credit report, title
services, recording charges, prepaid taxes and insurance, and other
closing costs. Some fees may be paid upfront and others at closing. The
lender should provide required disclosures, including a Loan Estimate
when applicable, so customers can review estimated costs.

Q13. How long does mortgage approval take? A: Timing varies based on the
completeness of the application, verification needs, property appraisal,
title work, underwriting workload, and the transaction’s circumstances.
The lender can provide an estimate for the specific application. The
chatbot should not guarantee a closing or approval date.

Q14. What is an appraisal, and why is it required? A: An appraisal is an
assessment of a property’s value for the lender’s purposes. It helps the
lender evaluate the property as collateral and may affect loan terms or
approval. The appraisal process and options for addressing a valuation
difference depend on the lender, loan program, and applicable rules.

Q15. Can I apply with a co-borrower? A: Many mortgage programs allow
eligible co-borrowers, but the lender determines who can apply and what
documentation is required. A co-borrower’s income, debts, credit
history, and other financial details may be considered under the
program’s rules.

Q16. Can I apply if I am self-employed? A: Self-employed applicants may
be eligible, subject to the lender’s underwriting requirements. They may
need to provide additional tax returns, profit-and-loss statements,
business records, or other evidence of stable qualifying income. The
lender will specify which documents are required.

Q17. What happens after I submit the application? A: The lender
generally reviews the application, requests supporting documents,
evaluates credit and repayment ability, orders or reviews
property-related services, and completes underwriting. The applicant may
receive requests for additional information. A final decision and any
conditions are communicated by the lender.

Q18. What is a Loan Estimate? A: In many U.S. mortgage transactions, a
Loan Estimate is a standardized disclosure that summarizes key loan
terms, projected payments, and estimated closing costs. It is not the
same as final approval. Customers should review the document carefully
and ask the lender to explain any terms or fees they do not understand.

Q19. What is mortgage insurance? A: Mortgage insurance may protect the
lender against certain losses if a borrower does not repay the loan.
Whether it is required, how much it costs, and when it can be cancelled
or ends depend on the loan type, down payment, applicable rules, and
loan documents. It is different from homeowners insurance, which
protects against covered property losses.

Q20. What if I cannot provide one of the requested documents? A: The
applicant should tell the mortgage team which document is unavailable
and ask whether an alternative is acceptable. Requirements depend on the
lender and loan program. The chatbot must not advise the customer to
alter, omit, or misrepresent information.

Q21. Does submitting an application guarantee approval? A:
No. Submitting an application does not guarantee approval or a
particular rate or loan amount. Approval is subject to the lender’s
underwriting, verification, property review, program requirements, and
any required conditions.

Q22. How can I protect my personal information during the mortgage
process? A: Submit documents only through the lender’s verified secure
portal or another channel the lender confirms is approved. Do not send
passwords, PINs, or one-time passcodes in chat or email. Verify
unexpected requests by contacting the lender through an official phone
number or website.

CHATBOT RESPONSE RULES - Provide general education only; do not promise
loan approval, a rate, a payment amount, a loan limit, or a closing
date. - Do not make a credit decision or infer eligibility from
incomplete information. - For personalized estimates, rates, fees,
required documents, and application status, direct the customer to the
lender’s current disclosures, secure mortgage portal, or loan officer. -
Never request passwords, PINs, one-time passcodes, or unnecessary
sensitive personal information in chat. - If a customer is in a live
application, encourage them to use the lender’s approved secure channel
to share documents.
